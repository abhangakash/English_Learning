TIP OF THE DAY

Networking conversations are not about what you say about yourself — they are about the quality of questions you ask others. The most memorable networkers are those who make others feel genuinely interesting. Prepare three deep questions before any professional event: 'What is the most challenging project you are currently working on?', 'What career decision has had the most impact on your development?', 'What is the one skill you wish you had developed earlier?'

🎧 1. Listening Activity : Professional Small Talk

🎯Focus
Professional Small Talk — how skilled networkers transition from casual to professional conversation.

"So I noticed you work in automation engineering — that is a fascinating field right now. What kind of systems are you currently working on? "I am particularly focused on [specific project or skill you are developing]."

📖 3. Reading Activity : Professional Networking

Reading Passage:

Professional networking is not a transaction — it is a relationship investment. The professionals who build the most valuable networks are not those who attend the most events, but those who follow up most consistently. A brief email the day after meeting someone, referencing a specific detail from your conversation, signals that you were genuinely listening. A connection who feels heard is far more likely to remember you and advocate for you than one who simply collected your business card. Advanced networkers also understand the concept of 'giving before getting' — they offer information, introductions, or help before making any request. This approach builds social capital that sustains careers across decades.

✍️ 4. Writing Activity : LinkedIn-Style Professional Bio

Write a 100-Word LinkedIn Bio

Include: (1) your specialisation, (2) a key skill or achievement, (3) what you are currently learning, and (4) what you are looking to contribute professionally.

💡 Writing Tip: Your LinkedIn bio should end with a specific invitation, not a vague statement. Instead of 'Looking forward to connecting', write 'I welcome connections from professionals in [specific field] who are working on [specific area].' Specificity attracts the right people.

🧠 6. Grammar Hack : Avoid Interrogative Tags in Formal English

📖 Why avoid question tags: Question tags ('isn't it?', 'na?', 'right?') are common in Indian English but sound informal and insecure in professional contexts.


❌ Incorrect: "The system works efficiently, isn't it?"

✅ Correct: "The system operates efficiently under normal conditions."


❌ Incorrect: "We should proceed, na?"

✅ Correct: "I recommend we proceed — do you concur?"


❌ Incorrect: "The data supports this, right?"

✅ Correct: "The data clearly supports this conclusion."


Rule: Replace 'isn't it?' with a confident statement. Replace 'na?' with 'Does that make sense?' or 'Would you agree?'
