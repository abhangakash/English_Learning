INFERRING AUTHOR'S PURPOSE

💬 "Reading is not receiving. It is a conversation with the writer's intent."
💡 TIP OF THE DAY

Before reading any article or document, ask yourself: 'Who wrote this, and why?' A government policy document serves a different purpose than a newspaper editorial, which differs from a product review. Identifying the author's purpose in the first 30 seconds of reading improves comprehension and critical evaluation of everything that follows.

📖 3. Reading : Interrogating Intent

Every text has a purpose — even texts that claim to be purely objective. The scientist who writes a research paper wants their findings accepted. The journalist who writes a news article has chosen which facts to include and which to exclude. Advanced readers do not receive text passively; they interrogate it. They ask: What does the author want me to believe? What have they left out? Who benefits if I accept this argument? These questions are the foundation of informed decision-making.

1️⃣ List three questions the author recommends asking of any text you read.

2️⃣ Apply these three questions to the promotional passage from the listening activity.

3️⃣ Find an engineering specification or product data sheet. Identify its purpose in one sentence.

✍️ 4. Writing Activity : Editorial Analysis

💡 Writing Tip: The difference between summary and analysis is this: summary tells WHAT the author said; analysis tells WHY they said it and WHETHER it is convincing. Your writing task today requires analysis, not summary — make sure your response answers 'why' and 'how effectively'.

🎯The Task
Read a newspaper editorial of your choice. Write 150 words analysing: (a) the author's main argument, (b) their apparent purpose, (c) one assumption they are making, and (d) one piece of evidence they could have included to strengthen their case.

💬IDIOM: "Read the room"

Meaning: Understand the mood and expectations of your audience

Example: "A skilled presenter reads the room and adjusts their tone based on audience reactions."

🧠 Grammar Hack: Avoid 'As Per' in Modern English

'As per' is a formal but increasingly outdated phrase, especially in spoken English. It sounds bureaucratic and clumsy.

❌ As per your instruction... → ✅ As you instructed...

❌ As per the specification... → ✅ According to the specification...

❌ As per my knowledge... → ✅ To my knowledge... / Based on my understanding...
