💡 TIP OF THE DAY

The fastest way to acquire advanced vocabulary is to learn words in clusters, not in isolation. When you learn 'mitigate', also learn 'mitigation', 'mitigating factors', and 'mitigation strategy'. One root word, learned fully in its family, is worth five isolated words on a vocabulary list.

📖 3. Reading : Vocabulary as a Precision Instrument

Technical vocabulary is not a barrier to understanding — it is a precision instrument. When an engineer says 'the system exhibits non-linear response characteristics under transient loading', they are not showing off. They are conveying in twelve words a concept that might take three paragraphs of plain English to explain — and with less precision. Advanced learners do not resist technical vocabulary; they invest in it deliberately. They read specifications, technical manuals, and journal papers with a vocabulary notebook — noting not just the word but its sentence-level context, its collocations (words it commonly appears with), and its grammatical behaviour.

1️⃣ What does the author mean by calling technical vocabulary a 'precision instrument'?

2️⃣ What does the author recommend noting in a vocabulary notebook beyond the word itself?

3️⃣ Define one technical term from your course with two collocations and a sentence.

🔥POWER WORD: PRAGMATIC (prag-MAT-ik)

Meaning: Dealing with things sensibly and realistically

Usage: "A pragmatic engineer finds the workable solution, not the ideal one."

💬IDIOM: "In the field"

Meaning: In a real, practical environment (as opposed to theory)

Example: "The prototype performed well in the lab, but real challenges only emerged in the field."

🧠 Grammar Hack: Avoid 'According to My Knowledge'

Avoid 'According to My Knowledge': This phrase is grammatically awkward and signals uncertainty in a way that reduces credibility

❌ According to my knowledge, the boiling point is 100°C.
✅ To my knowledge, the boiling point of water at sea level is 100°C.

❌ According to my understanding, the formula is...
✅ Based on my understanding, the formula is...

✅ Best phrase when uncertain: 'If I recall correctly...' or 'I believe... though I would like to confirm this.'
