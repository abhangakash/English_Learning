TIP OF THE DAY

When reading a technical article, do not start from the beginning. First read the abstract, then the conclusion, then the headings, and finally the full text. This 'top-down' approach means you already know the destination before navigating the journey — comprehension increases by up to 40%.

📖 3. Reading Activity · Academic English Structure

Reading Passage:

Academic English is not difficult because of its vocabulary — it is difficult because of its sentence structure. Academic writers frequently use nominalisations: converting verbs and adjectives into nouns to create formal, impersonal tone. 'The system failed' becomes 'A failure of the system occurred.' 'We analysed the data' becomes 'An analysis of the data was conducted.' Recognising nominalisations allows you to decode dense academic text rapidly. Advanced readers also pay attention to hedging in academic writing — words like 'may suggest', 'appears to indicate', and 'could potentially' that signal tentative conclusions rather than confirmed facts.

🧠 6. Grammar Hack: Avoid 'etc.' in Formal Writing

'Etc.' signals that the writer could not think of more examples. In formal and technical writing, either list all items or use a specific inclusive phrase.

❌ Incorrect: "The project involved planning, testing, analysis, etc."

✅ Correct: "The project involved planning, testing, and data analysis."


❌ Incorrect: "The team used sensors, actuators, controllers, etc."

✅ Correct: "The team used sensors, actuators, and microcontrollers, among other components."


Rule: If you cannot complete the list, write 'among others' or restructure the sentence.
