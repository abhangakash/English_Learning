📖 3. Reading Activity : Perfect Tenses in Professional Communication

Reading Passage:

Tense is not merely a grammar rule — it is a time machine for your listener. The present perfect tense ('we have completed') places an event in the past but emphasises its current relevance. The past perfect ('we had completed') establishes a sequence — one event before another. The future perfect ('we will have completed') projects a listener forward, creating anticipation and accountability. Engineers who master all three tenses communicate project status with a precision that simple past tense cannot achieve. 'We finished the test' gives information. 'We have finished the test, and the results are ready for review' creates urgency and readiness.

🧠 6. Grammar Hack: Correct Form of Perfect Tenses

📖 Memory Aid: have/has/had + PAST PARTICIPLE (gone, written, done, seen, made)


❌ Incorrect: "I have went to the lab."

✅ Correct: "I have gone to the lab."

❌ Incorrect: "She has wrote the report."

✅ Correct: "She has written the report."

❌ Incorrect: "We had did the test."

✅ Correct: "We had done the test."
