TIP OF THE DAY

Sub-vocalisation — saying words in your head as you read — limits your reading speed to speaking speed. Train yourself to read in visual chunks of 3–5 words rather than word by word. Your comprehension will dip initially, then exceed your previous level within two weeks.

📖 3. Reading Activity : Speed Reading Techniques

Reading Passage:

The two most powerful techniques for improving reading speed without sacrificing comprehension are chunking and pre-reading. Chunking means training your eyes to take in groups of words as single units rather than individual tokens. This exploits the brain's pattern-recognition architecture — our visual cortex processes shapes, not individual letters. Pre-reading means scanning headings, first sentences, and figures before reading in full. This creates a cognitive map of the text so that detailed reading becomes a process of confirmation rather than discovery. Both techniques are used by pilots, surgeons, and engineers who must process dense technical material under time pressure. Neither requires exceptional intelligence — only deliberate practice.

🧠 6. Grammar Hack: Train Forward Eye Movement

📖 Why avoid re-reading: Train Forward Eye Movement: Sub-vocalisation and regression (re-reading) are habits, not necessities. Reduce both deliberately


Exercise 1: Read a paragraph once. Cover it. Write what you remember. The first time you do this, you will miss 30–40%. After two weeks, you will miss less than 10%.

Exercise 2: When you read a difficult sentence and want to re-read it, force yourself to read the next sentence first. The context often resolves the confusion without regression.

Rule: Trust your first read. Re-read only when context fails completely.
