TIP OF THE DAY

The secret to great impromptu speaking is having pre-loaded structures, not pre-loaded content. If you know the PREP framework, the Three-Point Rule, and the Problem-Solution-Benefit frame, you can speak fluently on any topic — because the structure does the heavy lifting.

🎧 1. Listening Activity : Impromptu Structure

🎯Focus
Impromptu Structure — noticing how skilled speakers organise unrehearsed responses.
📝Listening Text
"You ask whether automation will eliminate engineering jobs. I would say no — and here is why. First, automation handles repetitive tasks; engineers create and maintain automation systems. Second, India's infrastructure deficit means engineering demand will grow for decades regardless. And third — perhaps most importantly — the engineers who understand both the technical and human dimensions of automation will be indispensable. So the future is not fewer engineers. It is differently skilled engineers."
📝Task: Map the Structure
Identify: Opening Position, Point 1, Point 2, Point 3, Conclusion and Notice how the speaker signals each with transition words.

🧠 Quick Listening Check

1. Does the speaker have a clear position from the start?


2. What transition signals the most important point?

📖 3. Reading Activity : Structured Spontaneity

Reading Passage:

Impromptu speaking is a practised skill, not an innate talent. Research at Toastmasters International shows that professionals who practise structured impromptu speaking for 15 minutes per day for 90 days become significantly more confident and coherent in unplanned speaking situations. The key is deliberate practice: not just speaking spontaneously, but practising structured spontaneity — knowing your frameworks so well that you apply them automatically. The mind that has a structure to reach for when under pressure never goes blank. Silence in impromptu speaking is not failure — it is a thinking pause. The most confident speakers in the world pause deliberately before major points. They are not struggling; they are demonstrating that their ideas are worth waiting for.

Meaning: Spoken without preparation; impromptu

Usage: "His extemporaneous response to the client's question revealed his deep expertise."

💡IDIOM: "THINK ON YOUR FEET"

Meaning: Respond quickly and intelligently without preparation

Example: "The ability to think on your feet in client meetings is what separates good engineers from great communicators."

🧠 6. Grammar Hack: Remove Filler Words

📖 Why to avoid filler words:

Filler words like 'um', 'uh', 'like', 'you know', 'basically', 'na' are placeholders for thinking time. Replace them with deliberate pauses.

❌ "The, um, the system, basically, you know, failed."

✅ "The system failed — [pause] — specifically in the power regulation module."


❌ "It works, na?"

✅ "Does that make sense?" or "Would you agree?"


Exercise: Record a 60-second monologue. Count your filler words. Try to reduce them by 50% over the next 7 days.

Tip: The most common Indian English filler is 'na' at the end of sentences. Replace 'it works, na?' with 'Does that make sense?' or 'Would you agree?'
